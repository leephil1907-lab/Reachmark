# Reachmark Digital — Client Onboarding SOP

**Document owner:** Founder / Operations  
**Applies to:** Every managed-service client, regardless of package  
**Standard workflow:** Contract and payment → Welcome and intake → Account access → Kickoff → Baseline audit → Technical setup → Approval → Launch → First-month review → Ongoing delivery

## Operating rule

A client advances only when the stage's completion evidence is recorded. A task that looks finished is not complete without the required proof. Silence is never approval. If a gate fails, keep the client in the current stage, record the blocker, assign an owner and due date, and tell the client what is needed.

## Client record required fields

Maintain one source of truth in the CRM or client workspace. At minimum, record:

- Client legal/business name, primary contact, decision-maker, account manager, purchased package and signed scope.
- Contract date, setup fee and first monthly payment status, recurring billing date, agreed advertising budget and who pays it.
- Sales-to-delivery handoff notes, including every promise made, exclusions, dependencies and known risks.
- Current stage, stage owner, due date, next action, blocker, last client update and evidence links.
- Goals, baseline values, definitions of enquiry / qualified lead / appointment / sale, reporting cadence and client approval contact.
- Required platforms, access owner, permission level, verification status and outstanding access blockers. Never store passwords or secret keys in this record.

## Stage-by-stage checklist

### 1. Contract and payment
**Owner:** Founder or account manager

- [ ] Signed agreement and statement of work (SOW) are saved.
- [ ] Scope, deliverables, exclusions, timing, dependencies and change-request process are clear.
- [ ] Setup fee and first monthly payment are confirmed received, or an explicitly approved billing exception is documented.
- [ ] Advertising budget, payment method and client responsibility are documented separately from agency fees.
- [ ] Account manager and client decision-maker are assigned.
- [ ] Client record, workspace and billing schedule are created.
- [ ] Sales-to-delivery handoff captures all promises and risks.

**Gate evidence:** Signed agreement + signed SOW + payment confirmation + completed handoff. No delivery work starts before this gate unless a written exception is authorized.

### 2. Welcome and intake
**Owner:** Account manager  
**Target:** Within one business day of activation

- [ ] Send one welcome email with primary contact, intake form, access checklist, kickoff booking instructions, proposed timeline and communication/approval expectations.
- [ ] Collect business goals, service area(s), priority services, audience, offers, seasonality and capacity constraints.
- [ ] Collect existing marketing history, known issues, brand assets and relevant policies.
- [ ] Agree initial success criteria and the available source of truth for reporting.
- [ ] Assign an owner and due date to every missing item.

**Gate evidence:** Intake is sufficient to plan the work, with outstanding items explicitly assigned. Do not silently treat missing information as supplied.

### 3. Account access
**Owner:** Operations coordinator; specialists verify their own platforms

Request only access needed for the purchased scope:
- [ ] Website/CMS and, only when required, hosting or DNS.
- [ ] Analytics, Tag Manager and Search Console.
- [ ] Google Business Profile.
- [ ] Advertising accounts.
- [ ] CRM, booking calendar and relevant communication tools.
- [ ] Shared brand-asset folder.

Security controls:
- [ ] Prefer named-user invitations and least-privilege roles; avoid shared passwords.
- [ ] Verify access by opening the platform and confirming the required capability works.
- [ ] Record who owns each account, granted role, verifier and any blocker.
- [ ] Never request credentials through ordinary intake forms or email.
- [ ] Client retains ownership/control of business assets. For Google Business Profile, use authorized representative/manager access where appropriate.

**Gate evidence:** Each required permission is tested and marked working—not merely invited. Missing access has an owner and due date.

### 4. Kickoff
**Owner:** Account manager and strategist  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes

- [ ] Confirm the priority business outcome and first service, location or campaign.
- [ ] Confirm scope, deliverables, exclusions, timeline and first milestone.
- [ ] Define enquiry, qualified lead, appointment and sale.
- [ ] Identify who answers incoming leads and expected response process.
- [ ] Name approvers for content, creative, budget and material changes.
- [ ] Confirm communication channel, reporting cadence, escalation path and feedback windows.
- [ ] Send a written recap with decisions, action owners, deadlines and open questions.

**Gate evidence:** Kickoff recap sent; priorities and responsibilities acknowledged; unresolved decisions assigned.

### 5. Baseline audit and first-month plan
**Owner:** Strategist

Review only channels included in the signed scope:
- [ ] Website and conversion paths.
- [ ] Local search presence.
- [ ] Existing campaigns and account structure.
- [ ] Tracking and attribution accuracy.
- [ ] Lead routing, response and follow-up.
- [ ] Available historical performance and data limitations.

Create a concise baseline and first-month plan:
- [ ] Record starting values, date range, source and measurement limitations.
- [ ] Separate verified observations from hypotheses and recommendations.
- [ ] List prioritized work, expected deliverables, owners and target dates.
- [ ] Agree which outcomes can be measured and which data the client must provide.

**Gate evidence:** Baseline report and prioritized plan are saved and shared; priorities are agreed. Never invent missing baseline numbers.

### 6. Technical setup
**Owner:** Relevant channel specialists

As applicable to the package:
- [ ] Landing pages or website improvements are configured.
- [ ] Campaign targeting and budget limits match the SOW.
- [ ] Conversion events and reporting are configured.
- [ ] Forms, booking links and CRM stages are connected.
- [ ] Lead notifications and routing reach the correct person.
- [ ] Acknowledgement and follow-up messages match approved copy and consent requirements.

Test the full lead journey:
- [ ] Visitor reaches the intended page.
- [ ] Test enquiry or booking submits successfully.
- [ ] Lead appears in the correct system with relevant source information.
- [ ] Intended person receives notification.
- [ ] Only approved response automation runs.
- [ ] Test event and outcome appear correctly in reporting, where supported.
- [ ] Record test date, result, evidence and any remaining limitations.

**Gate evidence:** End-to-end test results are recorded and critical failures are resolved. Never use a real customer’s personal data for a test submission.

### 7. QA and launch approval
**Owner:** Delivery reviewer and client approver

- [ ] Business details, offers, contact information and service areas are correct.
- [ ] Mobile pages, forms and booking paths work.
- [ ] Test leads reach the intended recipient.
- [ ] Tracking has been checked and known gaps are disclosed.
- [ ] Campaign budgets and account settings match the agreement.
- [ ] Copy and creative are approved by the designated client approver.
- [ ] Known limitations and dependencies are documented.
- [ ] Pause, rollback or recovery steps are known to the delivery team.
- [ ] Internal reviewer signs off.

Send one consolidated approval request with previews, exact launch scope, budget, outstanding limitations and requested decision date.

**Gate evidence:** Recorded affirmative client approval and internal authorization. No launch based on silence, implied consent or an unrecorded verbal “looks fine.”

### 8. Launch
**Owner:** Channel specialist and account manager

- [ ] Confirm approved pages/campaigns are live.
- [ ] Recheck lead delivery, notifications and tracking after launch.
- [ ] Monitor spend, technical errors and early lead relevance.
- [ ] Record launch timestamp, live URLs, settings changed and verification evidence.
- [ ] Send launch confirmation with what is live, what to expect, how to report issues and the next review date.
- [ ] Schedule the first-month review.

**Gate evidence:** Live state verified; launch record saved; client notified; first review booked.

### 9. First-month review
**Owner:** Account manager and strategist  
**Target:** Around day 30 after launch, adjusted to the engagement

- [ ] Summarize completed work against the SOW.
- [ ] Review actual spend separately from agency fees.
- [ ] Review available enquiries, lead quality and client-confirmed outcomes.
- [ ] Explain measurement gaps, small sample sizes and other limitations.
- [ ] Capture client feedback, operational issues and unresolved blockers.
- [ ] Agree next-month priorities, owners and due dates.
- [ ] Share the review notes and update the delivery plan.

**Gate evidence:** Review summary shared; next-month priorities agreed or explicitly assigned for follow-up.

## Standard CRM status board

Use these exact stages so every team member understands the same status:

1. Awaiting contract/payment
2. Intake pending
3. Access pending
4. Kickoff complete
5. Audit and plan complete
6. Setup in progress
7. QA in progress
8. Client approval pending
9. Ready to launch
10. Live
11. First-month review complete
12. Ongoing delivery

Every client-stage record must include **responsible owner, due date, blocker, next action, last update and completion evidence**. If the software cannot capture a field, track it in the approved client workspace until the CRM supports it.

## Escalation rules

- **Payment or scope unclear:** pause work and escalate to the founder/account owner.
- **Access missing:** keep the stage open; name the client-side owner and due date.
- **Tracking or lead delivery broken:** do not claim measurement is working; resolve or disclose before launch.
- **Client approval absent:** do not publish, spend or launch the pending change.
- **Budget mismatch or unexpected spend:** pause affected activity where safe and escalate immediately.
- **Material scope change:** document impact and obtain written agreement before starting additional work.
- **Personal data or account security concern:** stop the affected process, restrict access as appropriate, and follow the incident procedure.

## Definition of done

A client is not “onboarded” just because a welcome email was sent or a campaign was launched. Onboarding is complete when the first-month review is documented, outstanding issues have owners, the next plan is agreed, and the client transitions into ongoing delivery with a named account owner.
