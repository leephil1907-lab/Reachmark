# Reachmark Digital — HVAC Retainer Playbook

## Strategic focus

Reachmark Digital is being positioned as a specialist managed marketing agency for independent HVAC contractors. The initial market hypothesis is South Florida. Validate this through owner interviews before treating it as a proven niche.

**Positioning:** Reachmark helps HVAC companies improve local search visibility, website enquiry conversion, and lead follow-up, measuring the path from first contact to booked work.

## Commercial objective

Validate a recurring service model that could scale toward 17 clients and $86,000 in monthly retainer fees. This is an illustrative portfolio target, not a forecast or guarantee.

- Foundation: $3,000/month
- Growth: $5,000/month
- Scale: $7,500/month

These are proposed test prices. Confirm willingness to pay and delivery economics in discovery conversations. Client-funded advertising spend is excluded from agency revenue. Major website rebuilds and setup projects are separately scoped.

## Qualification criteria

Prioritize HVAC businesses that:
- Have dependable service delivery and capacity for additional jobs.
- Have a clear service area and preferred job types.
- Have someone accountable for answering calls and web enquiries.
- Can share baseline enquiry and sales information.
- Can fund the retainer and separate advertising spend.
- Will define and review lead quality and booked-job outcomes.

Defer prospects who demand guaranteed rankings or revenue, lack service capacity, refuse measurement, or expect unlimited custom work.

## Discovery questions

1. Which cities and ZIP codes do you serve?
2. Which jobs are most valuable and which do you want more of?
3. How many calls, forms, and service enquiries arrive monthly?
4. How many become qualified leads, appointments, and booked jobs?
5. What is the approximate gross profit per job category?
6. How quickly does the team respond to new enquiries?
7. Which marketing sources currently produce customers?
8. What marketing budget can be allocated separately from agency fees?
9. Who approves campaign budgets, offers, and website changes?
10. What would make a 90-day engagement worth continuing?

## Offer boundaries

- 90-day initial engagement proposed; monthly fees paid in advance.
- Written statement of work defines locations, campaigns, pages, monthly work limits, integrations, meetings, revision rounds, and reporting.
- Client owns ad accounts and pays media spend directly.
- Large website rebuilds and substantial integrations are separately scoped.
- No guarantee of ranking, enquiry volume, booked jobs, or revenue.
- Client retains ownership/co-ownership of its Google Business Profile and authorizes manager access as appropriate.
- Define lead, qualified lead, appointment, booked job, spam, duplicate, and out-of-area lead before reporting.
- Document access, approval, cancellation, offboarding, and handover terms.

## Evidence-led acquisition

Start with 100 qualified HVAC businesses in one service area. For each, capture company, service area, decision-maker, source of business contact, verified website observation, qualification notes, contact status, and opt-out status.

Lead with one observable issue and a testable recommendation. Do not claim a website issue caused a specific revenue loss without evidence. Use accurate sender identity and comply with applicable commercial outreach laws and opt-outs.

### Initial first-contact template

Subject: [specific contact or mobile issue] on [Business]

Hi [Name],

I'm [Your Name], founder of Reachmark Digital.

I reviewed [Business]'s website and noticed [specific, verified observation]. We help HVAC contractors improve the path from local search to a qualified service enquiry, then measure what happens after contact.

I can send a short explanation of the observation and how I'd test an improvement. Would that be useful?

[Full identity, commercial disclosure, postal address, and opt-out instructions as required]

## Delivery system

1. Baseline discovery and tracking audit.
2. Access and account ownership verification.
3. Conversion tracking QA before campaign changes.
4. Local search and campaign launch checklist.
5. Weekly lead-quality and delivery review.
6. Monthly scorecard: spend, enquiries, qualified enquiries, response time, appointments, booked jobs, and known gross profit where supplied.
7. Action log with owner and due date.
8. Renewal review based on evidence, economics, and capacity.

## Financial model

Use gross profit, not revenue alone, to assess marketing economics. Example only: a $5,000 agency fee plus $4,000 in media costs totals $9,000 before other incremental costs. At $1,500 gross profit per additional job, six jobs would cover $9,000, but this is not a performance prediction.

Track actual hours and direct costs per client from the first engagement. Reprice or narrow scope if delivery regularly exceeds the agreed allowance.

## First 30 days

- Interview 10–15 HVAC owners/operators.
- Validate the service area, buyer pain, and willingness to pay.
- Research 100 qualified prospects.
- Run a small, personalized outreach test.
- Aim for two paid engagements; treat this as a target, not a forecast.
- Record rejection reasons, delivery time, actual costs, and lead-quality visibility.

## Weekly scorecard

- Qualified prospects researched
- Personalized contacts sent
- Positive replies and opt-outs
- Discovery calls booked and held
- Qualified opportunities and proposals
- Signed clients and collected fees
- Delivery hours and direct costs by client
- Valid enquiries, appointments, booked jobs, and source attribution
- Overdue invoices, churn risk, and next actions
